Latin America

Expanding into Latin America

Latin America is one of the most dynamic regions for regulated cannabis, and one of the most varied. We are building training and licensing support market by market.

Why Latin America

Several countries now permit medical or scientific cannabis, and some allow licensed cultivation and export. Climate suits outdoor and greenhouse production, but each country has its own regulator, licence types and rules. Operators and investors need local knowledge and skilled teams, which is where training and licensing guidance help most.

Country snapshots

A plain-language summary from public sources. This is a starting point, not legal advice, and some of these markets are changing quickly.

Regulatory snapshot by country
CountryWhere things standWhat it means for our work
Colombia Medical and scientific use legal since Law 1787 of 2016. Licences cover seed use, cultivation (psychoactive and non-psychoactive) and manufacture of derivatives, handled by the Justice and Health ministries. The rules were reworked in 2021. The most developed licensing system in the region, so a strong fit for cultivation and extraction training and for licence acquisition.
Mexico Medical-use rules published in 2021 put production and research under COFEPRIS. In 2026 COFEPRIS stressed that court rulings allow personal-use permits but do not create a commercial market. Opportunities are limited to what is lawful today, such as research and pharmaceutical pathways. We are watching closely.
Argentina Law 27669 (2022) set a framework for medicinal cannabis and industrial hemp, and patients register through REPROCANN. Reports in 2025 said the national regulator was dissolved and its duties moved to other bodies, while some provinces push ahead. In flux. Provincial opportunities may exist, but we advise caution until the structure settles.
Uruguay Legalized cannabis in 2013 under a state-centered model with licensed producers. Established but tightly state-controlled. Training and compliance support may fit licensed producers.
Brazil, Chile, Paraguay, Peru, Ecuador Medical use is permitted in some form, with different speeds of implementation and access. Early-stage review. We will add detail as we validate each market.

How we are approaching the region

  1. Map the rules

    Country-by-country review of regulators, licence types, ownership rules and timelines.

  2. Build local relationships

    Work with local counsel, licensed operators and technical partners in each market.

  3. Pilot training

    Start with cohorts for licensed operators, adapting our programs to local crops and climates.

  4. Support licensing

    Add acquisition and transfer support where the law allows it.
